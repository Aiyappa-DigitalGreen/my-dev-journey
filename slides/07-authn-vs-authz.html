Backend · Auth · Topic 07

Who you are vs what you may do.

Authentication, authorization, and the one-digit bug that leaks customer data.

1 · The problem

A valid token isn't enough

"Show order #1001" + my token ✅  → mine → fine
"Show order #1002" + my token ✅  → someone else's
                                    name, address, phone ❌

The server knows exactly who I am. It still has to ask a second question.

Two checks

Both must pass

Check 1: token → who is this?        → Aiyappa ✅
Check 2: order #1002 → who owns it?  → Priya
         Aiyappa == Priya?           → ❌ refuse
2 · The picture

The hotel 🏨

  • Front desk checks your ID and gives a key card → Authentication: who are you?
  • The card opens Room 204 only → Authorization: what may you do?
  • Trick: auth-N = Name · auth-Z = Zone. AuthN always comes first.
Two different "no" answers

401 vs 403

  • 401 Unauthorized: "I don't know who you are." Missing or expired token. Log in again.
  • 403 Forbidden: "I know you, and you're not allowed." Re-login won't help.
  • ⚠️ 401 really means unauthenticated. It's a bad name from long ago.
💣 Real-world bug

IDOR

  • Insecure Direct Object Reference: the server checks the token but forgets the ownership check.
  • Change 1001 → 1002 and you see someone else's data. One of the most common real leaks.
  • Rule: check ownership on the server, on every request. Hiding a button is not protection.
🧭 Where & when

When to reach for it, and when not to

  • Where: orders, chats, statements, and roles (admin vs user).
  • When: any request asking for a thing by ID. Ask: "Did I check who owns this?"
  • When NOT: public data has no owner to check.
  • From scratch: write down who may see or change each piece of data first.
🎤 Q&A recall

Interview check

  • Q: AuthN vs AuthZ? A: Who you are vs what you may do. AuthN comes first.
  • Q: 401 vs 403? A: Not identified (log in again) vs identified but not permitted.
  • Q: Prevent IDOR? A: A server-side ownership check on every object access.
Recap

What we locked in

  • A token answers "who". The server must still ask "is this yours?"
  • Both checks pass → data. Either fails → 401 or 403.
