Backend · Auth · Topic 08

Never store the password. Store the smoothie.

Plain text, encryption, hashing, salt, and why slow is good.

1 · The problem

Check it without keeping it

  • Login compares what you typed with a stored value.
  • Stored as readable plain text? One leak exposes everyone.
  • 💥 RockYou, 2009: 32 million readable passwords. People reuse them, so Gmail and bank accounts fell too.
Idea 1 · Encrypt it?

The key is the weak spot

  • Encryption is reversible with a key.
  • The key has to live on the server, so the hacker steals it with the data.
  • 💥 Adobe, 2013: around 150M accounts. Anything decryptable can eventually be read.
2 · The picture

The one-way blender 🥤

Coorg@1234  →  a28ed1c3451948ba…
Coorg@1234  →  a28ed1c3451948ba…   same ✅
Coorg@1235  →  663a88b2e304d062…   totally different
  • A hash has no key and can't be reversed.
  • Login: hash what you typed, then compare with the stored hash.
  • That's why apps send a reset link. They don't know your password.
Key vs hash vs salt

Three different words

  • Key: unlocks encrypted data. Secret.
  • Hash: a one-way result. Can't be unlocked.
  • Salt: makes each hash unique. Not secret.
  • Password storage = hash + salt. No key anywhere.
Hacker trick

Blend guesses, compare

blend("123456")      → 8d969eef…  ❌
blend("password")    → 5e884898…  ❌
blend("Coorg@1234")  → a28ed1c3…  ✅ Aiyappa AND Ravi
  • Same password → same hash, so crack one and you get all of them.
  • Rainbow table: guesses blended once and reused on every leak.
The fix

Salt 🧂

x7Kq + Coorg@1234 → 87bab6ec…  (Aiyappa)
M2pz + Coorg@1234 → 819b5f17…  (Ravi)
  • A random value per user, blended in with the password.
  • Stored next to the hash, because login needs it.
  • Kills duplicates and rainbow tables.
3 · Slow on purpose

bcrypt / argon2 🐢

  • SHA-256: 1 billion guesses ≈ 1 second.
  • bcrypt: about 0.1 s per guess, so 1 billion guesses ≈ 3 years.
  • You hash once. The hacker hashes billions of times.
  • bcrypt stores its own salt: $2b$12$<salt><hash>
🧭 Where & when

When to reach for it, and when not to

  • Where: signup, login, "forgot password".
  • When: data you only need to check, never read back.
  • When NOT: data you must read back → use encryption. Never write your own hashing.
  • Hashing doesn't stop break-ins. It makes stolen data useless.
🎤 Q&A recall

Interview check

  • Q: How do you store passwords? A: A slow, salted hash (bcrypt/argon2). Never plain text, never encryption.
  • Q: Is the salt secret? A: No. It's for uniqueness, and it's stored beside the hash.
  • Q: Why not SHA-256? A: It's too fast. GPUs do billions of guesses a second.
Recap

What we locked in

  • Plain text ❌ → encryption ❌ → hash ✅ → + salt ✅ → slow ✅
  • Same input, same hash. That's how login works, and why salt is needed.
